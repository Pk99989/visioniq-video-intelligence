# Phase 2 benchmark — 2026-11-06

Adaptive sampling + dedupe, measured on this machine (Linux VM, CPU-only
YOLOv8n ONNX, hash embeddings, VLM provider `fallback` — captions are
the `detection` tier, so this measures sampling/detection, not VLM).

## Method

`scripts/phase2_bench.py`: upload each sample video through the real API
(`SYNC_PROCESSING=1`), count stored frames/scenes/detections, time the
whole request. Baseline is the v0.3.1 live run (1 fps, no dedupe).

## Results

| Video | Duration | Time | Frames kept | Scenes | Detections | Labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bus (still loop) | 10 s | **3.0 s** (was 9.1 s) | **5** (was 10) | 1 | **25** (was 50) | bus, person |
| moving traffic | 15 s | **11.6 s** | **27** of 30 extracted | 1 | **106** (was 61 at 1 fps) | bus, car, truck |

## Reading

- **Bus still video:** adaptive fps is 2.0 for a 10 s video (20 frames
  extracted), dedupe keeps one frame per 2 s gap → 5 frames. Same labels
  detected, 3x faster end to end, half the stored frames. No information
  lost: the frames were identical.
- **Moving traffic:** 30 frames extracted at 2 fps, dedupe dropped 3
  near-identical frames → 27 kept. More frames than the old 1 fps run, so
  more detections (106 vs 61) — denser sampling on a short video finds
  more vehicles entering the frame. That is the intended trade-off of
  adaptive sampling: short videos get denser coverage.
- **Scenes:** both videos segment into 1 scene with the mean-colour
  threshold (30.0). The traffic video has no hard cuts, so 1 scene is
  correct for this sample; scene splitting on gradual change is a known
  limitation of the colour-change detector, not of Phase 2.

## Config used

`ADAPTIVE_SAMPLING=1`, `ADAPTIVE_FPS_MIN=0.2`, `ADAPTIVE_FPS_MAX=2.0`,
`MAX_FRAMES=120`, `DEDUPE_SIMILARITY=0.995`, `DEDUPE_MAX_GAP_SECONDS=2.0`,
`SCENE_MERGE_SIMILARITY=0.99`. All are env-overridable.
